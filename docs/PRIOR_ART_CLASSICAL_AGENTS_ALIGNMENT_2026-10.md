## 1. Paper 1 (2609.33691) in numbers — §-anchored from the full text

Task: align classical texts with their translations segment-by-segment; seven systems (4 embedding pipelines, 3 LLM workflows — direct call / autonomous agent / agent + independent auditor) over **452 texts** in Pāli, Sanskrit, Mishnaic Hebrew, Tibetan = **9,833 human-aligned units** (§1; Sanskrit corpus = Itihāsa, Rāmāyaṇa + Mahābhārata verses with Dutt's translations, 2,499 units sampled, Apache-2.0, §3.2 Table 2).

- **Generative >> embeddings:** 93.4–93.9 % reference recovery vs 58.1–76.8 % for embeddings; smallest gap direct-vs-MITRA-E **16.5 pp [14.1, 19.1]** (§4.1, Table 5). Sanskrit corpus: generative **90.9–92.0 %**, embedding ceiling **98.8 %**, MITRA-E realized only **84 %** of the attainable, LaBSE **53.8 %** (§4.1–4.2).
- **The ceiling is structural, not semantic:** sentence-splitting aligners cannot express references that begin/end inside a sentence — Pāli ceiling just **70.2 %** overall (Dhammapada 42.6 %, Theragāthā 29.2 %, **Therīgāthā 10.8 %** — "a single English sentence often spans more than three verse lines", §4.2). In Sanskrit/Hebrew the ceiling was ~99 %, yet embeddings still recovered only 54–83 % — wrong links, not just unreachable ones (§5.1).
- **Agent vs direct on prepared passages:** **+0.54 pp [−0.02, 1.17]** (§4.3, Tables 6–7); per-corpus agent edge 1.05 pp Tibetan [0.05, 2.07], 1.04 pp Sanskrit **[−0.36, 3.11]**. What the agent *does* buy: structural validity **452/452 vs 437/452** for the direct call.
- **Auditor adds no established benefit:** audited−agent **+0.03 pp**; audit cleared 437/452 outputs at first review, gained 16 units / lost 13 = **net +3 of 9,833** (§4.4).
- **Reference mismatch ≠ error:** a blinded 3-judge panel labelled **77 %** of the direct call's 653 mismatches and **88–90 %** of the agent workflows' as *defensible editorial variation*; consensus major+minor errors only **1.44 %** (direct) / **0.70 %** (agent) / **0.61 %** (audited) of all units (§4.4, Table 8).
- **Long published documents (the headline condition):** 10 whole Pāli discourses as published online (translator's notes, headings; 1,486 units): whole-document recovery **direct 71.5 %** (8/10 valid docs) → **agent 84.3 %** (10/10) → **audited 92.1 %** (10/10). Cutting the same texts into **identical ~850-word chunks cut at source boundaries** converged all three to **93.4 / 93.5 / 93.6 %** — the direct call gained **+21.9 pp**, more than the entire agent+auditor ladder had added (§4.7, Table 12).
- **Cost of the ladder:** agent = median **9 model calls/text**, audited **16** vs **1**; prompt volume 75 M / 142 M tokens vs 2.1 M (~85 % cache hits); usage ≈**1.8×** and ≈**5.2×** the direct call; all 452 texts ≈ **≤ $6** at catalogue prices; median time 56 s / 80 s / 135 s (§4.6, §5.5).
- **Author's own doctrine (§6):** "a direct call is a reasonable baseline on manageable inputs, with structural validation and a policy for correcting invalid output … Decomposition (chunking) is promising for longer documents, **although the performance of automatically located chunks remains untested**." Public artifacts: [github.com/MateMetzger/when-do-agents-help](https://github.com/MateMetzger/when-do-agents-help).

## 2. Paper 2 (2609.14963) in numbers — §-anchored from the full text

Task: triage LLM translation errors **without references at inference time** — Pali→English; 3 translators (`deepseek-v4-pro`, `qwen3.6-plus`, `grok-4.3`) × **15,493 passages** = 46,479 candidates (§3.4); signals calibrated on a 3,000-item reference-informed LLM-adjudicated sample, validated on a **500-item author-adjudicated anchor** (Pali-trained author, §3.11, §4.7).

- **Source novelty = risk prior, not detector:** novelty alone captures ~48.7 % of panel-major at a 20 % review budget with **8.9 % precision**; the best transparent routing score (0.7 novelty + 0.3 peer-centroid) reaches **60.6 % @ 20 %**, still routing-grade, not error-grade (§4.2). Backtranslation adds almost nothing on top (AUC 0.820 → 0.832; recall 71.0 → 72.5 %, §4.3).
- **Strong no-reference GEMBA is the signal:** panel = gpt-5.5 + gemini-3.1-pro-preview + claude-sonnet-4.6; mean-risk **AUC 0.970** major-vs-nonmajor (score separation: panel-major mean GEMBA **69.3** vs minor **87.0** vs valid **95.2**); budget curve: **5 % → 59.4 % recall @ 82.0 % precision · 10 % → 81.6 % @ 56.3 % · 20 % → 94.7 %** (§4.4, Table 8). A single low score (any scorer ≤ 60) flags 4.5 % of the sample with **80.1 % major precision** — a cheap red-flag rule, §4.4.
- **Evaluator strength is load-bearing:** swapping to a "peer" panel (the translators' own class, self-scoring excluded) drops AUC 0.970 → **0.885** and 10 % budget recall 81.6 % → **61.4 %** (§4.6, Table 9).
- **Human anchor caps the claim:** vs author labels the LLM panel has major-error recall 93.4 % but precision **72.6 %** (overcalls minor; 3-class exact agreement 72.4 %, κ 0.559, §4.7 Table 11); strong-GEMBA AUC falls 0.970 → **0.924**, and the top 10 % captures only **47.3 %** of *author*-major errors (§4.8, §5.4). "The panel is a triage labeler, not an oracle."
- **Mixing signals after GEMBA hurts:** adding novelty/backtranslation to GEMBA *lowers* AUC (0.970 → 0.935 / 0.920) — cheap signals belong **upstream** as routing, never downstream as corrections (§4.5, §5.2).
- **Proposed workflow (§5.7):** source novelty → peer disagreement → strong-GEMBA on the review-eligible subset → route the high-risk tail to human review/retranslation; explicitly "a workload allocation system, not a replacement for philological expertise." Public artifacts: [github.com/MateMetzger/pali-translation-error-triage](https://github.com/MateMetzger/pali-translation-error-triage).

## 3. Verdicts keyed to named estate lanes

### V1 — ADOPT (doctrine): chunk-first + direct-call baseline — lane: **RussianTranslation** (`pwg_ru` paid runs, `gen_opt_harness2`)

Keep one card = one bounded request window with structural validation and the invalid-output policy we already run (`--stop-before-promote`, manifest v2 requeue). Do **not** add an agent tier or an auditor tier to the harness. Evidence mapping, concretely against our run shape:

- Our cards are prepared short passages — the paper's "identical chunks" regime, where direct/agent/audited differ by **0.2 pp** (93.4/93.5/93.6 %, §4.7). The agent's 0.5-pp edge has CI through zero on exactly this regime (§4.3); at 1.8× calls and 5.2× for audit it is the wrong spend under the no-PWG-window constraint (H811 requeue discipline) — every extra call per card widens the concurrency exposure we already throttle to `--max-wide=3 --stagger-ms=2000`.
- The paper's direct-call failure mode is structural invalidity (15/452 texts; 5/36 chunks, §4.3/§4.7) — precisely what `--stop-before-promote` + requeue handles, so the agent's one real advantage (452/452 valid) is already bought cheaper by validation than by agency.
- The direct call also needs a **retry-with-larger-output-allowance** policy for long outputs (their DN 5 needed a third request at 131,072 tokens, §3.6) — worth a one-line note in the harness runbook for oversized compound cards; not a new tier.

### V2 — ADOPT (doctrine): verse-bounded chunking before any LLM/agent spend — lane: **commentary corpus** (CommentaryStrategies, Sundarakāṇḍa Phase-2 commentator apparatus)

Never feed a whole commentary block to an aligner/judge in one call: cut at verse loci first (the Gita-Supersite per-verse HTML anchors our segmentation already uses are the exact analog of the paper's "chunks located with the reference", §3.6). Numbers to quote at design time: whole-document recovery degrades to **71.5 %** and only the agent+auditor ladder (84.3 → 92.1 %) claws it back — while identical chunks give a *direct call* **93.4 %**, i.e. chunking outperforms the entire ladder on unchunked input. Carry the paper's caveat verbatim: chunk location there used the reference, and "the performance of automatically located chunks remains untested" (§6) — our per-verse anchors ARE the reference-located chunking, which is why the doctrine transfers cleanly to the apparatus lane. For the commentator-apparatus dedupe vs Leonov/Kostina's 1,058 notes this also means: segment per verse before dedupe, not after.

### V3 — ADOPT (doctrine): budgeted strong-judge triage before human review — lane: **translation QA** (the `pwg_ru` G5/G6 review pipeline, [`RussianTranslation/src/build_g5_review_sheet.py`](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/RussianTranslation/src/build_g5_review_sheet.py), gold review queues [`gold_double_review_queue.py`](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/RussianTranslation/src/gold_double_review_queue.py))

Before minting a review sheet over N promoted cards, rank candidates with a no-reference judge panel drawn from a **stronger class than the translator tier** and route the top ~10 % to MG's review sheet. Budget math from the paper: top-10 % routing captures 81.6 % of panel-major errors at AUC 0.970 (§4.4) — and MG review is the scarcest resource in the estate, so concentrating it 10:1 is the point. Three guardrails the paper makes mandatory: (a) the judge class must exceed the translator class — a same-tier panel loses a third of the recall (81.6 → 61.4 %, §4.6); (b) panel/labels are triage, never a promoter — author-anchored top-10 % recall is only 47.3 % and panel major-precision 72.6 % (§4.7–4.8); (c) treat disagreement with the DE source / reference as *defensible variation* by default — paper 1's panel labelled 77–90 % of reference mismatches defensible (§4.4), which is the same epistemics FINDINGS §453 already encodes for PWG markers (ambiguous ⇒ leave unsplit and counted, never guessed).

### V4 — IGNORE: embedding-alignment machinery (Vecalign/Bertalign/MITRA-style) — lane: **RussianTranslation + commentary corpus**; named blocker: **task mismatch**

The estate lanes translate from printed sources with the source in hand by construction; there is no source↔translation alignment task to buy. And the paper shows the tool class is structurally wrong for our material even if the task existed: verse-boundary references cap sentence-based aligners below what any link choice can fix (Therīgāthā ceiling **10.8 %**), and on near-ceiling Sanskrit (98.8 %) the best embedding still realized only **84 %** of the attainable (§4.2). If a verse↔translation alignment need ever materializes (e.g. a reading-edition karaoke join), consume their public 452-text dataset + code instead of rebuilding ([when-do-agents-help](https://github.com/MateMetzger/when-do-agents-help)).

### V5 — IGNORE: an LLM auditor/reviewer tier between translator and human review — lane: **RussianTranslation** (`pwg_ru` promotion path); named blocker: **no established benefit at 5.2× cost**

Audited−agent recovery is **+0.03 pp** with a net of **+3 units across 9,833** (§4.3–4.4) — statistically nothing, on prepared inputs. The estate already runs a strictly stronger gate at the promotion boundary: machine validation + human-gated review (Opus review of promotions → G5/G6 sheets → MG). Per paper 2's own judge-dependence result, an LLM tier is only as good as its class gap to the audited system (§4.6); the human gate has an infinite one. Any future proposal to insert a reviewer-LLM before promotion must re-run paper 1's paired-output audit design on our data first, not cite this as precedent for adoption.

## 4. The chunk-first rule against OUR excerpt format (acceptance: on-our-data verdict)

RussianTranslation does not translate whole documents and never will: the unit is the **PWG card** — a headword's PWG records whose citation block is a set of **GRETIL-lineage stanza excerpts** (RV / epic verses quoted inside the entry) plus German sense glosses, translated card-by-card with the four-tier enumeration of [`microstructure.py`](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/RussianTranslation/src/microstructure.py) marking sense boundaries. Paper 1's Sanskrit corpus is the same shape taken to the corpus level — *Itihāsa* epic verses with a complete prose translation (2,499 units) — which makes its numbers read directly onto our format:

1. **A card already IS the chunk.** The paper's convergence regime is ~850-word windows cut at source boundaries; a PWG citation block is at or below that scale by construction. Rule: **never merge cards into a shared growing context to "save calls"** — that manufactures the whole-document regime where direct recovery collapses to 71.5 % and only agency (at 1.8–5.2× cost) buys it back; staying per-card gets 93.4 % from a plain direct call.
2. **Never split a card below the stanza.** The paper's ceiling analysis shows what happens when a mechanical splitter cuts across verse units: references become unreachable (Pāli verse ceilings 10.8–42.6 %). The analog failure for us would be chunking a card mid-sense or mid-stanza; the sanctioned cut points are the printed enumeration markers as read by `microstructure.py` — an ambiguous marker stays unsplit and counted (FINDINGS §453), never guessed.
3. **Oversized cards are the one exception.** If a compound card's citation block grows past the window, the paper's remedy is chunking, not a bigger model or an agent: cut at stanza boundaries and translate the pieces — this is exactly the +21.9-pp direct-call gain from Table 12, and it needs no new tier.
