## 2. Substance flags carried (not fixed)

1. **§5a "the single place" vs "seven".** §5a says PWG carries only seven comparative-grammar references and √*yabh* is one of them, then closes "the single place where Böhtlingk reached past the Sanskrit into comparative grammar is an obscene root". Read literally the two sentences contradict; presumably "the single place *within the obscene vocabulary*" is meant. One scope phrase would settle it — a claim edit, so left for the author.
2. **§3c table row order.** The table is introduced as "ordering the dictionaries by publication date", but CCS Cappeller (German, 1887) sits after CAE (1891) and AP90 (1890). Table rows are untouchable in this pass; moving the CCS row up two places is a one-line author fix.
3. **Two definitions of the obscene core.** §3a note and §3b list six terms (*futuere, cunnus, mentula, stercus, paedicare, mingere*); §3d's corpus sweep counts seven (adds *cacare*). That is why AP90 shows "— none —" in the §3a English table but "1 — one *cacare*" in §3c/§3d. The raw-vs-curated distinction is stated in §3d, but the differing term lists are not reconciled in words; a referee will ask. Author decision: either add *cacare* to the §3a/§3b list or say the sweep list is wider.
4. **§6 item 5 cites A33 and A34 by internal project IDs** ("sense-ordering", "Renou registers"). A BGS reader cannot resolve these; before submission they need either a real citation (if the sibling papers are out) or removal.
5. **Lanman dated 1906 in the diachronic table (§3c)** while the References give the Reader's first edition as 1884 (CDSL edition 1906). Placing LAN at 1906 in a table whose point is "fading after 1899" slightly muddies the shape; a "(1884)" beside the date, or a note, would pre-empt the objection.
6. **Wilson 1819** (carried from the review memo): the "absent before" baseline rests on the 1832 second edition; unchecked first-edition claim stays out, as the memo advised.
7. **§7 reproducibility:** "the scripts are not committed here (one-off analysis)". For a method the paper calls portable (§6 item 2) a referee may ask for the term list and filters as a file; the CSVs carry the results but not the extractor.
