## 2. Substance flags carried (not fixed)

1. **§1 claim 1 vs §3.6.** Claim 1 says the resource is «reproducible from committed code»; §3.6 and §6 say the builder «will not reproduce the shipped JSONL byte-for-byte». Both were kept verbatim. A human should decide whether claim 1 should read «regenerable from committed code» or cite the §3.6 contract explicitly.
2. **§2 venue sentence in the body.** «ComputEL … and LoResMT … are added below as submission-venue candidates alongside the existing Russian-orientalist target journals» is planning prose inside Related work; a referee will read it as an aside about where the paper is going. It belongs in the front matter `venue:` line. Left in place because it carries two counted URLs.
3. **§4.4 vs §4.5 sample counts.** Precision reports n=320; recall reports 287 sampled lemmata, n=280 in the denominator (267 covered + 13 missed) with 7 excluded. The arithmetic closes, but the abstract states «n=280 translated content lemmata» without the 287/7 split; a reader comparing abstract and §4.5 may ask. Unchanged.
4. **§6 rights bullet vs §8 H1458 paragraph.** §6 says the shippable slice «is undetermined until that documentation pass completes»; §8 says the alignment-only bundle is already mechanically emitted and «all 131 source works are still `needs_review`». Consistent, but §6 predates §8's paragraph and could point to it. Unchanged.
5. **116 works vs 131 source works.** §4.1 counts 116 distinct works in the lexicon; §8 speaks of 131 source works in the rights ledger. Presumably the ledger covers the whole corpus while the lexicon covers the harvested subset, but the paper does not say so. A one-clause explanation is a substance addition and was not made.
6. **§7 «unusually complete»** is a comparative with no comparison set named. Kept as the author's claim; a referee may ask for a comparator.
7. **Status-block TODOs.** Zenodo DOI, venue/byline confirmation, and the human gold packets remain open, as the status block says; readiness cannot move past 3/5 until at least the DOI and the human precision spot-check exist.
