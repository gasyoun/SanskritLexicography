## The options

1. **«сплошной»** — recommended, and the form already staged in the TSV. 95 + 98 pair hits in the main corpus and 16 + 15 in the 19th century, so it is attested with both heads in Böhtlingk's own century, and it is an adjective, which is what the German participle is. «Небо — одна сплошная туча»; «сплошным белым облаком» (Толстой, «Отрочество», 1854). It says exactly what «zusammenhängend» says of clouds: unbroken, forming one mass.
2. **«сгущающийся»** — the option MG's own 22-09 note pointed at, and the strongest collocate by sketch (nsubj #1 for туча, dice 8.94, 226 pair hits). Cost: it means gathering / thickening — a process, German `sich zusammenziehend`, not `zusammenhängend` — and the word is already spoken for. Row C06 (`fest —, consistent werden`) was approved on 22-09 as «сгущаться, становиться твёрдым —, плотным». Putting «сгущаться» in C07 too collapses two distinct German spans onto one Russian word inside the same review batch.
3. **«сомкнутый»** — semantically close (closed-up, joined). Corpus refuses it: 1 pair hit with туча, none with облако, none in the 19th century. One line only, and it is verbal there («туча сомкнула свою хмурь»).
4. **«сплочённый»** — what was proposed before the corpus was consulted. 0.94 ipm, a single pair hit in the whole main corpus, none in the 19th century. The card kills it.
5. **Keep «связный, последовательный»** — no change. Zero corpus support for either word with either head; the `unsupported_assertion` finding from the Codex Astra review stands unanswered.

**Recommendation: option 1, «сплошной».** It is the only candidate that is both semantically right for «zusammenhängend» and attested with both heads in the 19th-century slice. What would reverse it: a PWG usage where `zusammenhängend` of clouds means gathering rather than unbroken — that is the refutation criterion already written into the C07 row.
