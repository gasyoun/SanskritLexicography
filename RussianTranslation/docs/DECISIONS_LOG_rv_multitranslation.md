## 03-08-2026 (H2192) -- `added_by_one` never fires because it and `omitted_by_one` are ONE class, and the reason recorded for it was wrong

H1844 measured `added_by_one` at 0 of 12,000 and H1901 reproduced the zero on three independently-trained arms (0/300, 0/300, 0/267). Both entries ruled it "a prompt or taxonomy defect, not a fact about the Rigveda" and left the cause unmeasured; this entry measures it, with zero model calls, via `src/rv_added_by_one_diagnosis.py`.

**Cause: the two classes are converse readings of one undirected event.** `omitted_by_one` ("one rendering leaves material the other renders") and `added_by_one` ("one rendering supplies material with no counterpart in the other") are satisfied by the SAME configuration -- material present in a, absent in b -- read from opposite ends. The pair key is unordered (`a|b`), and the model reply shape was `{"class", "why"}` with no direction field. So a model that correctly saw surplus material on one side had no way to say WHICH side, and had to pick one of two names for one event. All arms picked the same one. The sharpest evidence that this was an oversight rather than a design: the DETERMINISTIC arm already emitted `missing_side` on every row it decided -- direction was expressible in this format all along and was dropped in exactly the one arm that cannot recover it any other way.

**The empirical half also corrects the reason both earlier entries gave.** They argued the zero was implausible *because Griffith 1896 pads freely with supplied and bracketed material*. Measured over the pilot's own 2,000 stanzas, Griffith is the LEAST-marked of the five witnesses -- 2 stanzas in 2,000 (0.1 %) -- because his padding is italicised in print and carries no delimiter after extraction. The padder in our data is Elizarenkova (71.7 % of stanzas carry parenthesised supplied material), then Jamison-Brereton (51.4 %) and Geldner (40.1 %). The verdict stands and gets stronger, not weaker: 8,744 pilot pairs carry a supplied-material marker on exactly one side, and `added_by_one` fired zero times against that population. A bracket is a proxy for supplied material and not proof of it, so this measures where the DIRECTION is textually recoverable -- which is precisely the population the class needs.

**The model was carrying the direction in prose the whole time:** 283 of the 283 model-decided `omitted_by_one` rows (100.0 %) name one of the pair's two translators in the free-text `why`. A deterministic backfill recovers the side for 235 of 286 asymmetric rows (82.2 %); 51 name both translators and are emitted as ambiguous rather than guessed; **zero** are unrecoverable.

**Second, independent defect found in passing:** `COARSE_MAP` sent `added_by_one` to `divergence` but `omitted_by_one` to `omission`, so the K3 coarse projection -- the fallback the taxonomy collapses to -- moved under a semantically vacuous relabelling. Recomputed on the three committed spike arms, old map vs converse-collapsed map: 0.235/0.235, 0.350/0.350, 0.216/0.216. Bit-identical, because the defect kept the unstable half unexercised. H1901's published coarse kappas therefore need no caveat and no re-issue, and those three numbers independently reproduce them.

**Conservative default taken:** fix the instrument, not the data. `surplus_side` is now mandatory on both asymmetric classes and must resolve to one of THAT pair's two translators (bare surname accepted; anything else recorded as a gap rather than coerced, the same posture H1901 took with out-of-enum classes); the prompt fixes the reading point at the first translator in the pair key so the two names denote different events; the deterministic arm emits `surplus_side` beside `missing_side`; `COARSE_MAP` sends both converse names to `omission`. The 2,000-stanza pilot was **not** re-typed -- that is a paid call this handoff is fenced against, and the recovered directions live in an additive sidecar (`pwg_ru/h2192/rv_divergence_direction_backfill.jsonl`) rather than mutating the committed pilot. Five test pins added, each verified RED on pre-fix master and GREEN after; `tests/test_rv_spine.py` 54/54, `window_selftest` 201/201, `lang_parity_check` 91 entries no drift. What is NOT claimed: that `added_by_one` will now fire -- proving that needs a re-typed spike. What is proven is that the class was undecidable as specified and is now decidable. Logged per the autonomy contract; did not stop, did not ask.

## 29-07-2026 (H1908) -- chronology is a first-class dimension, and wave 1's English witness is 118 years behind the standard

MG, 29-07-2026: "Griffith was the first, Elizarenkova the last, but lately the new English translation of the Rigveda is the very latest one. So the chronology matters a lot and must be noted and used." Both halves are acted on; neither was in the design.

**Half one -- ordering changes what a divergence MEANS.** The four run **Grassmann 1876–77 → Griffith 1896 → Geldner 1951–57 → Elizarenkova 1989–99**, and each later translator could read the earlier ones (Griffith worked from Grassmann and Wilson; Elizarenkova argues explicitly with Geldner and Renou). So a divergence between a later and an earlier rendering is **not a symmetric disagreement between peers** -- the later translator is often departing *knowingly*, having seen the earlier reading and rejected it. ARCHITECTURE Sec.3.5 defines the five classes purely pairwise and symmetrically, with no notion of who came first, so nothing in the taxonomy could express that. The gate sheet now carries a deterministic chronology band on every card (computed from publication years, never asked of a model -- these are facts), the two renderings are ordered **earliest-first** rather than in arbitrary pair-key order, and the band states the direction and the gap explicitly.

**Half two -- the English side is measured against Victorian English.** Griffith 1896 is our ONLY English witness, and the current scholarly standard in English is **Jamison–Brereton 2014** (OUP), which R4 deliberately excluded from wave 1 ("in copyright -- sample-scale reference use only, never bulk"). That exclusion was recorded as a rights decision and its *epistemic* consequence was never stated: every English-side finding in this layer -- the divergence classes, the TM tier's `griffith-en-1896` prior, any future PWG→EN gloss suggestion -- rests on a translation **118 years older** than the standard, made before most of 20th-century Vedic philology. 66 of the 100 gate cards involve Griffith, and each now says so on the card.

Consequences recorded rather than silently absorbed: (1) an English-side `semantic_shift` against Griffith is weak evidence of scholarly disagreement and strong evidence of Victorian rendering practice -- the reviewer is told this, not left to infer it; (2) the wave-2 paper cannot claim to compare *current* English scholarship without J–B, so its English claims are bounded to "Griffith 1896 vs the German/Russian tradition"; (3) admitting J–B even at sample scale is the single highest-value addition to the English layer and belongs in a scoped follow-up under R4's sample-scale limit, not as a quiet bulk ingest. Logged per the autonomy contract; did not stop, did not ask.

## 29-07-2026 (H1844) -- spike S2: the five-class taxonomy is NOT collapsed, and the spike alone would have got that wrong

VERIFICATION Sec.4's spike S2 asks whether `lexical_variant` and `semantic_shift` are actually distinguished or assigned arbitrarily, and K3 pre-authorises collapsing to a coarse three-class taxonomy (agreement / divergence / omission) **before** the pilot is spent if they are not.

The 50-stanza spike said collapse. On 300 model-decided pairs, `deepseek-chat` assigned `lexical_variant` **once** (0.3 %) against `semantic_shift` 62.0 %, with `added_by_one` and `omitted_by_one` at zero -- the textbook signature of a model using two of five labels. Taken literally that is K3 firing.

**The pilot says otherwise, and the pilot is right.** Over 12,000 model-decided pairs on 2,000 stanzas the distribution is `agreement` 37.2 % · `semantic_shift` 54.4 % · `lexical_variant` **6.0 % (719 labels)** · `omitted_by_one` 2.4 % · `added_by_one` 0.0 %. `lexical_variant` is used twenty times more often than the spike implied; 1/300 was small-sample noise, not a dead class. Collapsing on the spike would have discarded a live distinction and thrown away the very statistic wave 2's paper is meant to report. Recorded as a methodological caution for the next session: a 300-observation spike cannot settle the usage rate of a class whose true rate is ~6 % -- it will see zero or one and read that as death.

Decision: **the five-class taxonomy stands; no collapse.** Two caveats carried forward rather than buried. (1) `added_by_one` is genuinely inert -- **0 of 12,000** -- which is implausible on its face, since Griffith 1896 pads freely with supplied and bracketed material that the class exists to catch. That is a prompt or taxonomy defect, not evidence about the Rigveda, and it is the first thing the step-8 gate should be read against. (2) Whether `lexical_variant` and `semantic_shift` are *separable* -- as opposed to merely both used -- is still unmeasured: that needs the human gate (step 8, built and awaiting a vote) and the second, independently-trained model arm. The arm is specified and wired (`--provider openrouter`) but unrun: no `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` is reachable from this session, so inter-model kappa could not be computed. This entry is therefore a NOT-YET, not a verdict. Logged per the autonomy contract; did not stop, did not ask.

## 29-07-2026 (H1901) -- spike S2 answered on three model arms: the fine distinction is NOT separable, and the entry above was decided on the wrong statistic

The operator supplied an `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, so the second and third arms of spike S2 ran on the SAME seeded 50-stanza sample as the DeepSeek arm. Three independently-trained models, three vendors, two of them non-Chinese:

| Pair | five-class kappa | coarse kappa | `lexical_variant` vs `semantic_shift` kappa |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| `deepseek-chat` ↔ `openai/gpt-4o-mini` | 0.222 | 0.235 | **0.089** |
| `deepseek-chat` ↔ `google/gemini-2.5-flash` | 0.357 | 0.350 | **−0.012** |
| `openai/gpt-4o-mini` ↔ `google/gemini-2.5-flash` | 0.227 | 0.216 | **0.256** |

**Verdict: `lexical_variant` and `semantic_shift` are not being distinguished.** Kappa on that subset is 0.089, −0.012 and 0.256 — mean ≈ 0.11, one of them literally worse than chance. K3's collapse trigger has fired, on exactly the evidence VERIFICATION Sec.4 specified.

**The trap this caught, recorded because it nearly shipped.** Raw agreement on that same subset reads 89.0 %, 95.1 % and 85.7 % — which looks like near-consensus and is worthless. All three models overwhelmingly return `semantic_shift`, so they "agree" by defaulting to the majority class together; kappa corrects for that expected agreement and leaves almost nothing. Under extreme base-rate skew, raw agreement measures the skew, not the agreement. Any future gate in this repo that reports percent-agreement without a chance-corrected statistic is reporting the prior.

**This reverses the preceding entry, and the error is worth naming precisely.** That entry declined to collapse the taxonomy because the 12,000-label pilot used `lexical_variant` 6.0 % of the time — i.e. it argued from **usage rate**. Usage rate is not separability: a class can be assigned often and still be assigned unreproducibly, which is exactly what three arms now show. The earlier entry did flag the separability half as unmeasured and explicitly labelled itself a NOT-YET, so this is a completion rather than a contradiction — but the ruling it provisionally recorded was wrong and is withdrawn.

**Not collapsing blindly either.** The coarse three-class projection scores kappa 0.235 / 0.350 / 0.216 — better than the fine distinction, but still only "fair" on the Landis–Koch scale, not reliable. So the honest position is: the five-class taxonomy is not defensible as an inter-annotator-reproducible instrument; the coarse one is more reproducible but not yet demonstrated *reliable*. That is a question the step-8 human gate is the right instrument for, and it makes that gate MORE load-bearing than when it was generated, not less. The gate sheet is unchanged and still awaits a vote; the full 10,552-stanza run stays queued (R13).

**`added_by_one` is inert across all three arms — 0/300, 0/300, 0/267.** Three independently-trained models never once assign it. That is now strong evidence for the reading already recorded in H1844: a prompt or taxonomy defect, not a fact about the Rigveda. Griffith 1896 pads freely with supplied and bracketed material, so a class meant to catch exactly that firing zero times across three models indicts the instrument.

Two operational notes. `google/gemini-2.0-flash-001` was a **guessed** model id and 404'd for all 50 calls at $0.00 — the arm was re-run against `google/gemini-2.5-flash` after querying OpenRouter's live model list, and the failed rows were never reported as data. Look the id up; do not guess it. Gemini also returned 9 out-of-enum classes (267 of 276 model-decided labels usable), which the typer records as `class: null` rather than silently coercing. Spike cost across both new arms: **$0.054**. Logged per the autonomy contract; did not stop, did not ask.

## 29-07-2026 (H1844) -- STOP CONDITION 3: layer B fails the R14 bar on all three languages

Measured, not inferred. The 300-token frequency-stratified gold sample (`gold/rv_wordlevel_gold.jsonl`, seed 1844, 100 rows per target language, 75 per frequency stratum) was adjudicated by Opus 5 (`claude-opus-5[1m]`) over 69 read rows, and precision lands at **de 29.2 % (7/24) · ru 19.2 % (5/26) · en 10.5 % (2/19)** against a bar of 85 %. That is stop condition 3 as written in PLAN Sec.4: *"The layer-B gold sample scores below 85 % on all three languages -- that is not a parameter to tune blind; ship spine A alone, mark B `low_confidence`, and report."*

The failure mode is systematic rather than noisy, which is why more scale would not have rescued it. The aligner overwhelmingly returns the stanza's most salient proper noun whatever the source token was: `bāṇā́ḥ` ("arrows") -> `Brahmaṇaspati`, `kaláśe` ("in the beaker") -> `Soma`, `samaré` ("in battle") -> `Vrtra`, `dátravān` -> `Savitar`, `arcanā́nasam` -> `Soma`. The correct hits are concentrated in two easy classes -- a proper noun matching a proper noun (`rúśadgavi` -> `Ruśadgo`, `agnéḥ` -> `Agni`) and a high-frequency function word (`sá` -> `er`, `ayám` -> `He`, `yáthā` -> `dass`) -- i.e. exactly where an embedding needs no Vedic knowledge at all.

Swapping the alignment model does NOT fix it, and that was tested rather than assumed: a second 150-stanza arm under `sentence-transformers/LaBSE` (vs the committed `bert-base-multilingual-cased` default) reproduces the same signature -- 0 of 9,400 alignments below the 0.20 gate, 28.6 % mutual-argmax confirmed vs 30.2 %, the same [0.5,0.6) mass. So this is a property of subword-embedding alignment on transliterated Vedic, not of one checkpoint, and it is risk K1 landing exactly where VERIFICATION said it would.

Consequences taken, all of them the plan's own pre-registered response rather than a fresh judgment: (1) the **full-scale layer-B run is NOT executed** -- ~8.8 h of compute is not spent to scale a 20 %-precision signal; (2) layer B ships flagged `low_confidence` and is **excluded from the contradiction gate** (R14 marked default); (3) spine A is entirely unaffected and remains the deliverable (R5 designed layer B to be advisory precisely so its failure is survivable); (4) the 0.20 gate is **not** re-tuned to make the number look better -- with the pre-gate distribution massed in [0.5,0.6) a threshold could be drawn anywhere, and drawing it after seeing the gold is the blind tuning K2 and R14 exist to forbid. Honest limitation on the measurement itself: n = 69 of the drawn 300 were adjudicated, so the per-language n is 19-26; at an observed ~20 % the 95 % upper bound stays far below 85 %, so the verdict is not in doubt, but the point estimate is coarse and the remaining 231 rows are left `verdict: null` rather than assumed. Logged per the autonomy contract; did not stop the run, did not ask.
