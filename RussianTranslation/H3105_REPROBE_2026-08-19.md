H579 read its 173.1 s as "the first movement toward the ceiling" and warranted

straddle a **~175 s plateau** — the host is ~5 % *slower* than yesterday, well
inside noise. So H579's apparent 1.6× gain over H532 was largely the ~20 %
smaller rebuilt payload, not a faster host. The series is flat, not descending.

Worse for launch purposes: at 182 s a card sits **just above** the 180 s
`KILL_CEIL`, so cards would be killed outright rather than merely slow —
strictly worse than H579's position at 173 s. Lane stays **PAUSED**.

### Retire the trivial-call trigger

The precondition's cheap trigger — *an unrelated Sonnet `agent()` call
returning in seconds* — **fired green and was wrong**: `wf_fa941fe1-ed3`
returned in 6,823 ms and the real-card probe then took 182,302 ms, a 27×
spread. Trivial-call latency has no predictive power over schema-carrying
real-card latency, which is the same lesson H442 already learned about
bare-text load-representative probes (H442: a 3.3 s trivial probe passed, the
launch still degraded; H462/H532: a 24.5 s bare-skeleton probe passed, real
cards exceeded 180 s). This is now the third variant of the same trap.

**Successor rule:** re-probe only after a **multi-week** gap, or when an
unrelated **schema-carrying** call on a comparable payload comes back fast.
Do not spend a probe on the strength of a fast trivial call.

## Two operational notes for the successor

1. **A Workflow script generated on Windows is rejected before any agent
   fires.** Two launches failed with `script contains control characters that
   would be hidden in the approval dialog` — Python's `write_text` had emitted
   CRLF, and `\r` is a control byte. Neither rejection spent the probe. Write
   the script with `write_bytes(js.encode("ascii"))` and verify with
   `tr -d '\012' < f | tr -d '\040-\176' | wc -c` → 0. Checking with
   `read_text` hides this, because universal-newline translation converts
   CRLF to LF on the way in.
2. **The handoff's "`SERVER_OUTAGES.md` row 29" is stale numbering.** The
