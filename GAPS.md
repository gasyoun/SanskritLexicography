_Created: 08-07-2026 · Last updated: 07-09-2026_

**Epistemic sibling of [`FINDINGS.md`](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/FINDINGS.md).** FINDINGS is what is *known*. This file is its **negative space** — the act FINDINGS cannot hold: **not-yet-knowing**, the frontier of things we have explicitly NOT measured. The moment a gap is measured, it **graduates** to a FINDINGS row (delete it here, cite the finding there). One of the seven episteme registries minted under [H356](https://github.com/gasyoun/Uprava/blob/main/handoffs/archive/H356-Opus_csl-corrections_epistemic-sibling-registries_08.07.26.md); the full set is on the [episteme dashboard](https://gasyoun.github.io/SanskritLexicography/episteme/). Its infra twin is [`Uprava/GAPS.md`](https://github.com/gasyoun/Uprava/blob/main/GAPS.md).

**How to read a row.** Every row opens with two glyphs:

- **Importance dot** (identical scale to FINDINGS): 🔴 3 high · 🟠 2 medium · 🟡 1 minor — here the dot rates the **value if measured** (what it would unblock).
- **Origin marker:** ⚙️ auto (a set-difference script emitted this — a dataset with no FINDINGS row) · ✍️ human (a session flagged it).

Then `Why it matters:`, `Blocker:`, `How to close:`, and a `> **Source:**` line.

### §2. Error population of the 26 dictionaries whose two correction eras never overlap
🟠 ✍️ **We have NOT estimated the error-prone-record population for 26 of 42 dicts — and the "use a different estimator" route is now closed by measurement, not still open.**
Why it matters: correction-campaign planning assumes a convergence it cannot demonstrate wherever no estimate exists.
Blocker: **data, and now provably only data.** [H3986](https://github.com/gasyoun/Uprava/blob/main/handoffs/H3986-Opus_csl-observatory_error-population-low-recapture-estimator_03.09.26.md) (06-09-2026) scored four candidate estimator families against PW, MW and BUR — the dictionaries where Chapman *is* computable — under a rule fixed before the numbers were read. Every estimator that borrows strength across dictionaries fails: pooled prevalence ×0.62–×3.37 of the known answers, correction-density extrapolation (RECIPES §6) ×0.35–×2.98, two-era Chao2 ×1.02–×2.33, corrector Chao2 ×0.96–×2.17. The cause is structural: error-site prevalence across the three spans a factor of 4.1, so the exchangeability every pooled estimator needs is false. An empirical-Bayes shrinkage estimator passed the rule and was rejected on a diagnostic showing its prior contributes under 7% — it is Lincoln–Petersen in disguise and needs exactly the overlap these dictionaries lack.
What the same work *did* close: the m ≥ 10 floor is a precision convention, not an identifiability boundary. Chapman below it is median-shifted by a factor that depends on sample size and barely on the dictionary (×0.54 at m=1, ×0.69 at m=2, ×0.88 at m=5; the three agree within 9% at every rung). Dividing that measured shift out gives **5 dictionaries their first bounded estimate** (`skd`, `stc`, `ae`, `mwe`, `inm`) and labels **7 more unproofread** on the record-count cap. That is why this row shrank from 40 to 26 rather than closing.
How to close: **a second correction occasion for a below-floor dictionary** — a targeted re-proofing pass over an already-corrected sample, a few hundred records, enough to move it off m = 0. No further estimator work will do it; that has been tested. A covariate explaining the prevalence spread (source medium, scan quality, entry length) could rescue the pooled family, but needs a larger ground truth than three dictionaries first. Owner csl-observatory [`error_recapture_lowm.md`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-observatory/blob/main/reports/error_recapture_lowm.md) + [`error_recapture.md`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-observatory/blob/main/reports/error_recapture.md) (paper A48).
↔ Interlinks: [RECIPES §6](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/RECIPES.md) (correction loci census) is the per-dict correction-density base the extrapolation candidate was built on — and the candidate it grounds was **tested and refuted** here (×0.35–×2.98), so the census stands as a census, not as a route to this estimate · [ASSUMPTIONS §6](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/ASSUMPTIONS.md) (a correction queue stays valid) is the perishability that makes a second era hard to accrue.

### §14. Which `-ī` stems are the monosyllabic type, and which `-at` citations are genuine `-ant` stems?
✅ ✍️ **GRADUATED IN FULL 06-09-2026 ([FINDINGS §630](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/FINDINGS.md) + its 06-09-2026 addendum, [VisualDCS PR #123](https://github.com/gasyoun/VisualDCS/pull/123) and [PR #126](https://github.com/gasyoun/VisualDCS/pull/126)) — both pools are measured across their WHOLE token mass, NOUN and ADJ alike, on an external lexical signal.**
Why it mattered: the `-ī` bucket of the nominal grid (E51, 65,332 tokens) mixed the polysyllabic devī/nadī type with the monosyllabic śrī/strī/dhī type, which takes different endings in the strong cases — so its per-cell ending list was a blend of two paradigms presented as one. The `-ant` bucket (48,074) likewise pooled `-ant`/`-vant`/`-mant` with the master's own `-at`/`-vat`/`-mat` citations of the same stems (`bhagavant` and `bhagavat` are two `lemma_id`s), hiding how many distinct stems there really are.

What is MEASURED — the ADJ half (H4011, per-lemma from `dcs_full.sqlite` read-only, the substrate G2 does not carry): `-ant` ADJ is **36,768 tokens over 1,880 lemma_ids**, splitting **1,015 / 34,809** one-lexeme-two-spellings, **0 / 0** two headwords, **193 / 661** `at_only`, **13 / 95** `ant_only`, **659 / 1,203** unresolved; the `-ī` class's **664 ADJ tokens / 54 lemma_ids** split **3 / 31** monosyllabic against **51 / 633** polysyllabic. No NOUN or G2 total moved: all eleven reconciliation rows pass, and the two halves partition cleanly — **zero lemma_ids carry both upos**.
The pinned pair is answered: DCS `bhagavat` is `lemma_id` **48482**, tagged **ADJ** — exactly why G2's NOUN-only universe could not see it — and PWG indexes भगवत् and भगवन्त् under the identical entry-id pair `53806,80057`, so it is **one lexeme under two spellings**. Across all 1,880 ADJ lemma_ids **not one** comes out `two_headwords`.
Residual (named, not a blocker): **659 lemma_ids / 1,203 `-ant` ADJ tokens — 3.3 % of the ADJ mass, 2.5 % of the pool — stay pooled** because they have no witness in either MW or PWG under either spelling.
