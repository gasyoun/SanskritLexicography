3. **Keying — not parsing — is the estate's dominant defect class.** A machine key silently drops or merges data in five measured shapes: cross-scheme joins (IAST↔SLP1 naive match selects on diacritics and flatters the result — 82.7 % vs 56.2 % verb-root mass, [FINDINGS.md:4857](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/FINDINGS.md) §487); scheme pluralism inside one corpus (DCS lemma maps keyed SLP1 vs IAST, [ASSUMPTIONS.md:29](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/ASSUMPTIONS.md) §2); lossy normalization (NFD+strip erases length/retroflex and collides with pitch accent, [DEAD_ENDS.md](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/DEAD_ENDS.md) §5); non-unique ids (`OccId`/`sent_id` silently dropped 20 tokens / 449 sentences, DEAD_ENDS §6); homonym-index assumptions (giant verb roots at index > 0 — 23 of 236 have none at 0, [ASSUMPTIONS.md:50](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/ASSUMPTIONS.md) §3). The sanctioned counter-key is `sanskrit-util`'s length-preserving `form_key`/`to_slp1`, and the sanctioned counter-rule for Cyrillic is a **lookup table, never a character rule** (622-row `cyrillic_proper_noun_slp1.tsv`, zero rule-derived keys, [GAPS.md:75](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/GAPS.md) §6).
